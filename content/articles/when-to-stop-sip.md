Stop an SIP when the goal it was started for is reached or within two to three years, when the fund no longer fits your written plan, or when your income has taken a real hit. Do not stop it because the market fell, because a neighbour has a better idea, or because someone is pitching a new product. This guide separates the good reasons from the bad ones, explains when to pause rather than stop, and shows how a once-a-year review replaces reacting to the news.

## The three good reasons

An SIP exists to serve a goal. The reasons to stop it come from the goal, not from the market.

The first is that the goal is close. If the money is for a house deposit in 2029 or college fees in 2028, the last two to three years are not the time to leave the whole amount exposed to equity swings. The SIP has done its work.

The second is that the fund no longer fits the plan. Perhaps its mandate changed, perhaps it drifted into a category you did not choose, or perhaps your plan changed and the mix of equity and debt you need is different now. This is a planning decision, made calmly, against the written plan.

The third is an income shock. A job loss, a long illness, a business that has slowed. If the EMI, the rent and the school fees are under pressure, the SIP is the right thing to pause, well before the insurance premium or the emergency fund.

## The three bad reasons

Markets fell. This is the most common reason people stop, and the one that costs the most. An SIP buys units every month. When prices fall, the same ₹10,000 buys more units. Stopping when prices are low means you bought at the high prices and skipped the low ones, the reverse of what the SIP was built to do.

A neighbour's tip. A colleague's cousin has a scheme, a stock, a plot, or a trading app. If your plan did not include it last year, a conversation at a wedding is not a reason to include it now.

A new product pitch. Someone calls about a scheme with a "limited window". Any product worth owning will still be there after you have slept on it and checked it against your plan.

> Stop an SIP for a reason that comes from your plan, never for a reason that comes from the news.

## Pause versus stop

Most fund platforms let you pause an SIP for a few months without cancelling it. Use this when the reason is temporary: a medical bill, a gap between jobs, a quarter of thin business income. Pausing keeps the mandate alive and avoids the friction of starting again, which many people never get round to.

Stop outright only when the reason is permanent: the goal is reached, or the fund has been replaced in the plan. Even then, stopping the SIP is not the same as redeeming the money. The units you already hold stay invested until the plan says otherwise.

| Situation | Pause or stop |
|---|---|
| Three months of tight cash | Pause |
| Goal is 2 to 3 years away | Stop and start shifting |
| Fund no longer fits the plan | Stop, redirect the SIP |
| Market has fallen sharply | Neither, continue |

## Moving money as a goal nears

When a goal is two to three years out, the task is to protect what has been built. The usual approach is to move the money in steps, not all at once, from equity funds into lower-volatility options such as debt funds or fixed deposits. Some families move a fixed slice every quarter over the final years. The point is that the amount you need on the date you need it should not depend on what the market does in the last six months.

Tax matters here. Redemptions from equity funds held more than a year are taxed differently from short-term ones, and the rules change. Check the current year's rules, or ask us to, before you set the schedule.

## Review once a year, with a plan

Most SIP decisions go wrong because they are made in the moment: a headline, a message in a family group, a red number on an app. The fix is boring. Pick one month each year, sit down with the written plan, and ask four questions for each SIP:

- Is the goal still the same, and is the date still the same?
- Does this fund still fit the role it was given?
- Has my income changed enough that the amount should go up or down?
- Is the goal now within three years, so that it is time to start moving?

If the answers are no, no, no, no, the SIP continues and you close the file. If you have had a raise, raise the SIP. That is the whole review. Everything outside that one month is noise.

## What to do this week

- List every SIP you run, with the goal it serves and the year that goal falls due. If an SIP has no goal written against it, give it one.
- Check whether any goal is now within three years. If so, note it as the first thing to discuss at your review.
- Set one calendar reminder, twelve months out, titled "SIP review", and do nothing else to the SIPs until then unless income changes.
